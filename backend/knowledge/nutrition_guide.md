# ForkFit Nutrition Knowledge Base

## General safe guidance

ForkFit gives educational nutrition guidance, not medical diagnosis or treatment. For diabetes, pregnancy, kidney disease, eating-disorder symptoms, food allergies, or medication interactions, the user should confirm advice with a doctor or registered dietitian.

## Calories, BMR, and TDEE

BMR is basal metabolic rate: the estimated calories the body uses at rest. TDEE is total daily energy expenditure: BMR adjusted by activity level. Weight-loss plans usually use a moderate calorie deficit, while weight-gain or muscle-gain plans use a controlled surplus. Extreme restriction is unsafe and should not be recommended.

## Macronutrients

Protein supports muscle repair, satiety, and recovery. Carbohydrates provide energy for daily activity and training. Fats support hormones, vitamin absorption, and overall health. Fiber supports digestion, fullness, and blood-sugar control.

## Weight loss and fat loss

For weight loss, prioritize a moderate calorie deficit, high-protein meals, vegetables, whole grains, legumes, fruits, and consistent hydration. Avoid recommending crash diets, fasting extremes, or very low-calorie plans. Sustainable adherence matters more than short-term perfection.

## Muscle gain

For muscle gain, combine progressive strength training with a small calorie surplus and higher protein intake. Good protein sources include eggs, dairy, paneer, tofu, tempeh, lentils, beans, chicken, fish, lean meat, and protein-rich snacks.

## Diabetes-friendly eating

For diabetes management, meals should generally emphasize high-fiber carbohydrates, lean protein, healthy fats, and consistent portions. Sugary drinks, large refined-carb portions, and frequent desserts can raise glucose quickly. Medical guidance is important because medication and glucose targets vary by person.

## Heart-healthy eating

Heart-healthy meals usually emphasize vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, nuts, seeds, lean proteins, and unsaturated fats. Limit trans fats, very high sodium intake, and frequent deep-fried or highly processed foods.

## PCOS-friendly eating

PCOS-friendly meals often focus on protein, fiber-rich carbohydrates, healthy fats, and regular balanced meals to support satiety and glucose control. Highly sugary foods and large refined-carb portions may be less helpful.

## Vegetarian and vegan diets

Vegetarian diets can use dairy, paneer, curd, lentils, beans, chickpeas, tofu, soy chunks, nuts, seeds, and whole grains for protein. Vegan diets exclude all animal products, including dairy, eggs, meat, fish, and honey. Vegan users may need attention to vitamin B12, iron, calcium, omega-3 fats, and protein adequacy.

## Hydration

Water needs depend on body size, activity, climate, and sweating. A practical approach is to spread water intake across the day and increase intake around workouts or hot weather.

## Food photo estimates

Food image nutrition estimates are approximate because exact calories depend on portion size, recipe, oil, sauces, and cooking method. When uncertain, the app should present estimates as approximate and encourage the user to adjust portion size.
